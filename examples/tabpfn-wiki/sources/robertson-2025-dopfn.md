Introduces **Do-PFN**, a TabPFN-style Prior-Data Fitted Network (7.3M parameters) pre-trained purely on synthetic data to perform causal effect estimation via in-context learning. Whereas TabPFN predicts *observational* outcomes, Do-PFN also simulates *interventions*: it samples millions of structural causal models, draws an observational dataset and interventional query points from each, and trains the transformer to predict interventional outcomes. At inference it takes an observational dataset plus a query $(x, \mathrm{do}(t))$ and in a single forward pass predicts the conditional interventional distribution $p(y \mid \mathrm{do}(t), x)$ — with no knowledge of the causal graph and, notably, without requiring the unconfoundedness assumption. Published at NeurIPS 2025.

## Key claims

- **def-dopfn**: Do-PFN is "a foundation model pre-trained on data from structural causal models (SCMs) that can predict interventional outcomes and causal effects from observational data," performing ICL to predict interventional distributions with no causal-graph knowledge. (§1, Fig. 1, p.2–3) ^def-dopfn
- **def-cid**: The central estimand is the conditional interventional distribution (CID) $p(y \mid \mathrm{do}(t), x)$, conditioning on covariates $x$ and answering "what is the distribution of outcomes given features $x$ and intervention $\mathrm{do}(t)$." (Eq. 1, §2, p.3) ^def-cid
- **cate-from-cid**: CATE is derived from CIDs as $\tau(x) := \mathbb{E}[y \mid \mathrm{do}(1), x] - \mathbb{E}[y \mid \mathrm{do}(0), x]$ — a difference of expectations under the predicted CID; the $\mathbb{E}[y\mid\mathrm{do}(t),x]$ terms are conditional expected potential outcomes (the paper does not use the acronym "CEPO"). Treatments are binary $t \in \{0,1\}$. (§2, p.3; §4.3, Eq. 6, p.7) ^cate-from-cid
- **train-prior-scm**: The training prior samples datasets from SCMs — a DAG is topologically sorted, each node an additive-noise model $f_k(z_{\mathrm{PA}(k)}, \epsilon_k) = (\sum_l w_l z_l) + \epsilon_k$ with nonlinearities drawn uniformly from $\{x^2, \mathrm{ReLU}, \tanh\}$ and Kaiming-style weights; observational size $M_{\mathrm{ob}} \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(10, 2200)$. (App. C, p.19; Algorithm 1, p.5) ^train-prior-scm
- **train-intervention-sim**: For interventional points the same noise $\epsilon_k$ used to draw the covariates $x_k$ is held fixed while $\mathrm{do}(t_k)$ is applied and $y_k$ sampled from the intervened SCM ("can also be seen as simulating counterfactuals or single potential outcomes"); pre-training repeats over millions of SCMs. (App. C, p.20) ^train-intervention-sim
- **thm-forward-kl**: Proposition 1 — SGD per Algorithm 1 minimizes the expected forward KL divergence between the true CID and the model $q$, yielding a forward-KL-optimal CID approximation w.r.t. the SCM prior. (§3, Eq. 3, p.5; proof App. A, p.16) ^thm-forward-kl
- **no-unconfoundedness**: Unlike the potential-outcomes framework, Do-PFN's method "also includes scenarios without the unconfoundedness assumption"; positing a prior $p(\theta)$ over SCMs (hence over graphs) extends classical do-calculus, which fixes a single graph/SCM. (§3, p.4) ^no-unconfoundedness
- **uncertainty-decomp**: Eq. 4 decomposes the posterior predictive into three uncertainty sources — (i) aleatoric (SCM noise), (ii) unidentifiability $p(\theta \mid r)$, the distribution over observationally equivalent SCMs in an equivalence class, and (iii) epistemic $p(r \mid D_{\mathrm{ob}})$, uncertainty about the Markov-equivalence class from finite data. (§3, p.6) ^uncertainty-decomp
- **thm-consistency**: Proposition 3 (Consistency) — as $|D_{\mathrm{ob}}| \to \infty$ the epistemic term vanishes and the posterior CID concentrates on the observational- (Markov-) equivalence class $[\theta_0]$; if $\theta_0$ is identifiable ($[\theta_0]=\{\theta_0\}$) it recovers the true CID. Proof uses Doob's theorem / martingale convergence and ideas from Balazadeh et al. (CausalPFN). (§3, Eq. 5, p.6; App. B, p.17–19) ^thm-consistency
- **front-back-door**: Six synthetic case studies require different adjustments per the front-door and back-door criteria (Observed Confounder, Observed Mediator, Confounder+Mediator, Unobserved Confounder, Front-Door, Back-Door); Do-PFN "correctly perform[s] the appropriate form of adjustment without explicit knowledge of the causal graph… as if applying the front-door and back-door criteria." (§4.1–4.2, Fig. 2, p.6–7; App. Table 1, p.21) ^front-back-door
- **arch-histogram**: Architecturally a TabPFN-style transformer (7.3M params), specialized only by an indicator marking the first column as treatment; the output is a bar-distribution (histogram / discretized Riemann distribution), whose discretization error is a stated calibration limitation. (§3, p.5; §4.4, p.8) ^arch-histogram
- **dont-pfn**: "Dont-PFN" is a control PFN trained on the same prior but to predict the *observational* PPD $p(y_{\mathrm{ob}} \mid x_{\mathrm{ob}}, D_{\mathrm{ob}})$; the large Do-PFN vs Dont-PFN gap is "empirical evidence that our pre-training approximates something fundamentally different from just a standard posterior predictive distribution of observational outcomes." (§4.2, p.7) ^dont-pfn
- **results**: Do-PFN significantly outperforms Random Forest, TabPFN v2, and Dont-PFN in CID prediction, and outperforms meta-learners (S/T/X-Learners), a causal-forest DML approach, and deep methods DragonNet and TARNet in CATE and ATE estimation; significance via Friedman/Nemenyi tests. (Fig. 3, §4.2–4.3, p.7–8) ^results
- **ood-robustness**: Do-PFN is robust to unseen noise distributions (Laplace, Student-T, Gumbel, mixtures) and to different non-linearities, but degrades sharply with doubled Gaussian noise variance; v1 degrades on large graphs (21–50 nodes) while v1.1 (trained up to 60 nodes) recovers. (Fig. 4, §4.4, p.8–9) ^ood-robustness
- **common-effect-rct**: A "Common-Effect" case study "perfectly represents a randomized controlled trial (RCT)"; there, standard tabular regressors (TabPFN v2, Random Forest) match Do-PFN, because with no directed edge from treatment to covariates there is no distribution shift between context $D_{\mathrm{ob}}$ and query. (§4.2; App. E.4, Fig. 16, p.27) ^common-effect-rct
- **calibration-picp**: Calibration is measured via Prediction Interval Coverage Probability (PICP), NOT Integrated Coverage Error; Do-PFN is well calibrated, slightly under-confident on identifiable cases (bar-distribution discretization + imperfect forward-KL), and correctly raises uncertainty on the unidentifiable Unobserved-Confounder case. (§4.4, p.8; App. E.5, Fig. 18) ^calibration-picp
- **realcause**: On the RealCause semi-synthetic benchmark (where unconfoundedness is enforced, favoring baselines) Do-PFN v1.1 is strong on 3 of 4 datasets and Pareto-optimal in speed vs accuracy; on two known-graph real datasets (Amazon Sales, Law School) it is best on Law School and competitive on Amazon Sales, outperforming others in mediated scenarios. (§4.5, Fig. 6, p.9–10) ^realcause
- **limitations**: Stated limitations — (1) generalization depends on the synthetic SCM prior adequately capturing real causal complexity, with largely-synthetic validation; (2) amortized inference has different, still-developing statistical guarantees vs non-amortized estimators; (3) scope is restricted to binary interventions and i.i.d. data (counterfactuals, other intervention types, non-i.i.d. inputs are future work). (§5, p.10) ^limitations

## Notes

- Do-PFN and CausalPFN (Balazadeh et al. 2025) are concurrent NeurIPS 2025 papers. Key contrast: Do-PFN targets the full interventional *distribution* $p(y\mid\mathrm{do}(t),x)$ including aleatoric noise and does *not* enforce unconfoundedness/identifiability; CausalPFN targets the *expectation* $\mathbb{E}[Y_t\mid X]$ and builds identifiability into its prior.
