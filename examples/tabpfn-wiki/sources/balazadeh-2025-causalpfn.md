Introduces **CausalPFN**, a single ${\sim}20$M-parameter PFN-style transformer that amortizes causal effect estimation. Trained once on a large library of simulated data-generating processes (DGPs) that satisfy strong ignorability, it estimates causal effects on new observational tabular datasets in forward passes, with no per-dataset training or tuning. It ports the TabPFN recipe — in-context learning approximating a Bayesian posterior predictive over a rich synthetic prior — from prediction to causal inference. Its target estimand is the Conditional Expected Potential Outcome (CEPO) $\mu_t(x) = \mathbb{E}[Y_t \mid X = x]$, from which CATE and ATE follow. Its central theoretical result: CEPO posterior predictives consistently recover the true CEPO *if and only if* the prior is CEPO-identifiable — which the authors guarantee by constructing an ignorability-satisfying prior. Published at NeurIPS 2025.

## Key claims

- **def-causalpfn**: CausalPFN is a ${\sim}20$M-parameter PFN transformer that amortizes causal effect estimation across simulated DGPs, estimating effects on new observational datasets in forward passes with no per-dataset training, tuning, or hyperparameter search. (§1; Fig. 2; §6) ^def-causalpfn
- **def-cepo**: The primary estimand is the Conditional Expected Potential Outcome (CEPO): $\mu_t(x) := \mathbb{E}[Y_t \mid X = x]$; $\mathrm{ATE} = \mathbb{E}[\mu_1(X) - \mu_0(X)]$ and $\mathrm{CATE}(x) = \mu_1(x) - \mu_0(x)$ follow directly. (§2, Eqs. 1–3, p.3) ^def-cepo
- **potential-outcomes**: CausalPFN works in the potential-outcomes framework and targets the *expectation* $\mathbb{E}[Y_t \mid X]$ (removing aleatoric outcome noise), NOT the full interventional distribution $p(Y \mid \mathrm{do}(T), X)$; the full interventional distribution is Do-PFN's target. (§2, p.3; App. E, p.40) ^potential-outcomes
- **asm-ignorability**: Strong Ignorability is the central identifying assumption (Assumption 1): (i) $Y_t \perp T \mid X$ for all $t$ (unconfoundedness) and (ii) $P(T=t\mid X) > 0$ a.e. (positivity); stated as untestable in practice. (§2, p.3; §7, p.10) ^asm-ignorability
- **cepo-identifiability**: CEPO-Identifiability (Definition 1) — $\mu_t$ is CEPO-identifiable when it can be written as a functional of the observational distribution $P_{\mathrm{obs}}$; different DGPs can induce the same $P_{\mathrm{obs}}$ but different causal effects, so estimation is impossible without identifiability. (§2, p.3; App. A) ^cepo-identifiability
- **thm-consistency**: Proposition 1 — under mild regularity, as $|D_{\mathrm{obs}}| \to \infty$ the mean of the CEPO posterior predictive converges almost surely to the true CEPO *if and only if* the prior is CEPO-identifiable; proof uses an "observational quotient space" of DGPs sharing $P_{\mathrm{obs}}$ plus Doob's consistency theorem. Ignorability is the practical way to secure identifiability. (§3, p.4; App. B.1, p.28) ^thm-consistency
- **cepo-ppd**: CEPO-PPD (Definition 2) — the posterior predictive distribution of the CEPO, obtained by pushing the implicit posterior $\pi(\theta \mid D_{\mathrm{obs}})$ through $\mu_t(x;\theta)$; a concentrated CEPO-PPD means the data is informative, capturing epistemic uncertainty about the effect. (§3, p.4; App. A, Eq. 16) ^cepo-ppd
- **loss-causal-prior**: Causal Data-Prior Loss (Definition 3, Eq. 9) — $L_t(\theta) = \mathbb{E}[-\log q(\mu_t(x;\theta) \mid x, t, D_{\mathrm{obs}})]$; minimizing it is equivalent to minimizing the expected forward-KL between the true CEPO-PPD and $q$, needing only prior samples (no posterior sampling). (§3, p.4; App. C, Prop. 4) ^loss-causal-prior
- **loss-histogram**: Histogram Loss / Quantized Distribution (Eq. 10) — the outcome axis is discretized into $L = 1024$ bins over $[-10, 10]$; query tokens are projected to $L$ logits and softmaxed to a quantized distribution; the true CEPO is smoothed by a narrow Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(\mu_t(x), \sigma^2)$ integrated over bins, and cross-entropy minimized. It approximates the causal data-prior loss and coincides as $\sigma \to 0$, $L \to \infty$. (§4, p.6) ^loss-histogram
- **prior-construction**: The synthetic prior over DGPs: retrieve a base table from 337 OpenML tables (or synthesize via TabPFN-v1 random networks), select covariates $X$, relabel two columns as $\mu_0(X), \mu_1(X)$, add zero-mean noise to get $Y_0, Y_1$, generate treatment logits $f(X)$ (RCT-constant / linear / non-linear), draw $T \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\mathrm{Sigmoid}(f(X)))$, set $Y := Y_T$. Because $T$ depends only on $X$, strong ignorability holds by construction and the sigmoid guarantees positivity; ground-truth CEPOs are accessible for the loss. (§4, Fig. 4, p.5; App. D.1, p.31) ^prior-construction
- **heterogeneity-control**: A heterogeneity hyperparameter $\eta \sim \mathrm{Unif}[0,1]$ rescales CATE variance while preserving the ATE ($\mathrm{Var}[\tau\mid x] = \eta^2 \mathrm{Var}[\tau_{\mathrm{raw}}\mid x]$): $\eta=0$ gives homogeneous (zero-variance) CATE, $\eta=1$ the original heterogeneity; a separate $\gamma \in [0,1]$ controls positivity by exacerbating extreme propensities. (App. D.1, Eqs. 41–45, p.31–32) ^heterogeneity-control
- **rct-mechanism**: RCT simulation appears as one of three treatment-logit mechanisms in the prior ("Randomized treatments (RCT)": constant $f$, uniform propensities, treatment independent of covariates); evaluation also uses five real marketing RCTs whose Qini curves give unbiased top-quantile ATE estimates. The exact phrase "common effect" is not used. (App. D.1; §5, p.7–8) ^rct-mechanism
- **arch-transformer**: A 20-layer PFN-style transformer encoder, hidden size 384, QK-RMS-normalization, parallel SwiGLU FFN, ${\sim}20$M parameters, no positional encoding; asymmetric attention masking means both context and query tokens attend only to context tokens, so CEPO-PPD predictions are mutually independent; covariates padded to $F=100$. (§4, p.6; App. D.2, p.33) ^arch-transformer
- **training-protocol**: Two-stage training — (i) a predictive phase mimicking TabDPT-style PFN training (AdamW, warmup+cosine, context length 16K), (ii) a causal phase optimizing the CEPO loss (schedule-free optimizer, context length 2,048); compute roughly four A100 GPUs for $\leq 1$ week plus two days on an H100 (checklist states single A100 for 7 days for the base model). (App. D.2, p.33) ^training-protocol
- **metric-ice**: Integrated Coverage Error (ICE, Eq. 13) — $\mathrm{ICE}_\mu := \int_0^1 (\mathrm{cov}_\mu(\alpha) - \alpha)\, d\alpha$ for regression and $\mathrm{ICE}_\tau$ for CATE; negative values mean overconfidence. Across all model–DGP pairs $\mathrm{ICE}_\mu \le \mathrm{ICE}_\tau$; since $\mathrm{ICE}_\mu$ is computable from observational $(t,x,y)$ while $\mathrm{ICE}_\tau$ needs the unobservable true CATE, temperature-scaling logits to drive $\mathrm{ICE}_\mu \to 0$ also calibrates or makes conservative the CATE intervals. (§5, Eq. 13, p.8; App. D.7) ^metric-ice
- **ood-calibration**: Synthetic polynomial (degrees 1–4) and sinusoidal DGP families with Gaussian/Laplace/Uniform noise test in- vs out-of-distribution; CausalPFN is calibrated/conservative in-distribution but severely overconfident on OOD DGPs (outside prior support), and regression-ICE temperature scaling removes the overconfidence even OOD. (§5, p.8; App. D.7, p.36–39) ^ood-calibration
- **results-table1**: Across 310 realizations (100 IHDP, 10 ACIC 2016, 100 Lalonde CPS, 100 Lalonde PSID), CausalPFN attains the best average CATE rank ($2.30 \pm 0.10$ by PEHE) and competitive ATE rank ($4.45 \pm 0.19$); IHDP PEHE $0.58 \pm 0.07$ (best). Baselines: Forest DML, DR-Learner, T/S/X/DA-Learners (EconML), TarNet, DragonNet, RA-Net, IPW, BART, GRF — most tuned via FLAML/grid-search. (Table 1, §5, p.7) ^results-table1
- **vs-tabpfn**: Plain TabPFN (regression output as a CEPO proxy) is "surprisingly competitive" without causal tuning; CausalPFN beats it on every benchmark except ACIC 2016, and fine-tuning TabPFN on CausalPFN's causal prior improves it — confirming the value of identifiable causal priors. (Table 3, §5, p.9–10) ^vs-tabpfn
- **vs-dopfn**: Head-to-head, CausalPFN beats Do-PFN on all four benchmarks (e.g. IHDP PEHE $0.58$ vs $6.07$; ACIC $0.92$ vs $4.11$); the stated reason is that Do-PFN "does not explicitly enforce identifiability," so per Proposition 1 its posterior predictive cannot in general concentrate on the true effect. (App. E, Table 8, p.40) ^vs-dopfn
- **limitations**: Stated limitations — (i) fundamentally assumes strong ignorability (untestable, no validity guarantees without it); (ii) theory needs a well-specified prior and asymptotically large data, no finite-sample theory; (iii) performance degrades on the largest tables due to the PFN size-scalability trade-off; (iv) implemented only for binary treatment; (v) limited to ignorability/back-door — instrumental variables and richer priors are future work. (§7, p.10) ^limitations

## Notes

- Concurrent with Do-PFN (Robertson et al. 2025).
